- **MWDY2 binning error in `seqtest()`.** `seqtest(condition = "MWDY2")` can stop with "With `block`, D and Y must not need binning: raise Dsubsets/Ysubsets ..." (check at `R/montest.R` ~1193). The check counts distinct values of the stacked treatment (D1 in block 1, D2 in block 2, i.e. their union), but `seqtest()` sets `Dsubsets = max(4, uniqueN(D1), uniqueN(D2))` (`R/seqtest.R` ~197 for KRDY2, ~226 for MWDY2), which is smaller than the union when D1 and D2 take different values. A candidate fix (`uniqueN(st[[st_T]])`) was tried and reverted; revisit, and also consider whether MW with `block` needs the check at all (the Y part of it is KR-only, and blocks use D as is).

- **AHS-style and MW-style variants of FSDY/FSSY? (open)** `seqtest()`'s FSDY/FSSY put Y inside the moment (summed KR cells per Y-bin, `kr_sums`; joint event `(d1-d2)1{Y in b}`, `(d1+d2-1)1{Y in b}`). Two alternatives: (a) AHS-style: `montest(condition = "AHS", linearD = TRUE)` on the constructed treatment, Y a forest feature, Q = D with no Y event, so the first stage is conditional on Y (denominator P(Y in S | Z) depends on Z) - a different moment from the cells, not just a different set search, so unlike MW vs KR the two need not agree in population; AHS is not sharp for the LATE assumptions (the cell family can reach KR sharpness, pooled sums are weaker implications). Blockers: AHS's binary-D guard (`R/montest.R` ~1352) rejects the {0,1,2} constructed treatment (relax for `linearD = TRUE`), and the identification of the conditional-on-Y first stage for D1 -/+ D2 needs checking against the AHS paper. (b) MW-style: keep the joint-event moments but let Y be a forest feature that learns the sets b on the training half (as MW does for interior D values); the true MW analogue of FSDY. Decision so far: keep cells as FSDY/FSSY; revisit (b) if simulations show learned sets matter, (a) only as a separately named condition.

- **Residualize Y for the KR-type conditions? (open)** `Y.res` (cross-fit E[Y|X,FE] residual, `R/montest.R` ~1802) is applied only where Y is a forest feature (MW, AHS; MWD/MWDY/MWDY2 in `seqtest()`). Where Y builds the pseudo-outcome Q (KR in `montest()`; KRD*/KRDY/KRDY2/FSDY/FSSY in `seqtest()`), Y is binned raw (`montest()` ~1303-1338; `seqtest()` pre-bins with `binarize_var`). Residual sets `{Y - m(X) in A}` are valid (m is a function of X/FE only), so this affects power, not size. Raw global bins mean different parts of the within-X distribution in different X-regions, so with covariates and Y depending on X, KR's sets A line up poorly; this may explain part of the MW vs KR power gap in simulations (not tested). Plan if pursued: opt-in `Y.res` for KR (default unchanged, since results would change), residualize and then bin; `seqtest()` must residualize before its own pre-binning or leave binning to `montest()`; residualize only Y, never D2 (it is part of the cell). Open: which sample/fold the residual is fit on (MW fits within margin cell and sample half), and whether MWDY should residualize D2 as an outcome at all (currently does, apparently). First check: simulate KR with/without residualized Y and MW with `Y.res = FALSE` on X-dependent-Y designs.
